In-Wall Cord Routing

Running cables inside the wall gives the cleanest look but requires cutting into drywall. This can typically be done in existing construction using an in-wall rated power kit and cable channels.

In some cases — near fireplaces, with thicker walls, or in certain home construction types — in-wall routing may not be possible or practical. Surface cord covers are a clean, code-compliant alternative.

TV Mounting FAQs

Do I need to supply the mount?

You can, or we can source one for your TV size and use case. If you already have a mount, just let us know the brand and type when you reach out.

What if studs aren't where I need them?

We can use a mounting board to bridge studs, or select a bracket placement that works with the stud layout while still getting the TV where you want it.

Can you mount a TV above a fireplace?

Yes. Above-fireplace mounting requires a tilting bracket and secure fastening into the wall structure. We handle this regularly.

Can you do other jobs in the same visit?

Yes. TV mounting is often combined with picture hanging, shelf installation, or other small jobs in one visit.

TV Mounting in Moon Township and Pittsburgh Area Homes

TV mounting looks straightforward until you actually start doing it. The studs aren't where the math says they should be. A wall that looks like standard drywall over wood framing turns out to be plaster, or drywall over concrete block. The TV is heavier than expected. The mount you ordered doesn't line up the way the diagram suggested. By the time most people work through the complications, they've got a wall full of holes and a TV that's still not up. We do this regularly enough that none of those things slow us down.

In Moon Township homes, the most common TV mounting calls are for living room and bedroom installs — typically in homes where the owners have just moved in, just bought a new TV, or are finally getting around to a project that's been sitting on the list. Older homes in Moon Township sometimes have plaster walls or unpredictably spaced studs, which changes the approach compared to standard drywall. We check the wall type and stud layout before drilling anything so the mount ends up where it needs to be, secured to something solid.

When we mount a TV, we locate the studs, confirm the wall can support the mount and TV's combined weight, and position the mount at the right height centered on the viewing area. We run through cord management — either surface-mounted cord covers or in-wall cable routing depending on the setup and wall type — so the installation looks clean from across the room. Everything gets checked for stability before we leave, and we walk you through how the mount adjusts if it's a tilting or full-motion style.
