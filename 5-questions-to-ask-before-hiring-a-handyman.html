5 Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Handyman

May 2026 — Handled Home, Moon Township, PA

Letting someone into your home to do repair work is a real act of trust. Most homeowners in Moon Township, Coraopolis, and across the Pittsburgh area have had at least one experience with a contractor who didn't show up, didn't finish, or left things worse than they found them. A few minutes of due diligence before hiring can save you a lot of frustration and money.

These five questions won't make you an expert in hiring, but they will quickly separate the professionals from the people you should pass on.

Question 1: Are You Insured?

Why it matters

This is the most important question on the list and the one most homeowners skip. If an uninsured handyman damages your property — breaks a pipe, cracks a tile, puts a hole through a wall they shouldn't have touched — you have little recourse beyond arguing with them directly. If they get injured on your property while working without insurance, you could be looking at a liability claim against your homeowner's policy.

Insurance protects both of you. It means the handyman has a professional stake in doing things right, and it means you're not left holding the bag when something unexpected happens on the job.

What a good answer looks like

A simple "yes, fully insured" isn't enough. Ask what type of coverage they carry (general liability at minimum) and whether they can provide a certificate of insurance before work begins. Any professional handyman will have this ready and won't hesitate to share it.

Red flag: Any handyman who gets defensive about the insurance question, changes the subject, or says something like "I've never had a problem" is telling you exactly what you need to know. Walk away.

Question 2: Can You Provide References or Reviews?

Why it matters

Anyone can claim to do good work. Reviews and references from real customers are the only way to verify that claim before you hire. A handyman with a track record in your neighborhood — actual jobs completed for homeowners in Moon Township, Coraopolis, Sewickley, or nearby Pittsburgh suburbs — has something to show for their reputation that words alone don't provide.

This matters particularly for work that's hard to evaluate in the moment. A drywall patch might look fine the day it's done and show poor feathering under certain light a week later. Reviews written after the job is complete and the paint is dry give you a more honest picture.

What a good answer looks like

A handyman with real experience will point you toward Google reviews, social proof, or be willing to provide references from recent customers. Be skeptical of someone who only has testimonials on their own website — those are curated. Search for them independently before committing.

What to look for in reviews: Specific details about the work done, mentions of communication and reliability, and how the handyman handled anything that went wrong. Generic five-star reviews with no detail ("Great job! Highly recommend!") carry less weight than specific descriptions of the experience.

Question 3: Do You Provide Written Estimates?

Why it matters

Verbal estimates are the source of more contractor disputes than almost anything else. "I thought you said it would be $300" versus "I said around $300 depending on what I found" is a conversation that happens constantly in home repair. A written estimate eliminates ambiguity before it becomes a problem.

A written estimate also tells you something about how a handyman operates. Someone who tracks the scope, price, and expectations of a job in writing is approaching it professionally. Someone who prefers to keep things vague and verbal should prompt you to ask why.

What a good answer looks like

Before any work begins, you should have a clear, written description of what's included, what materials are covered, and the price. Understand what would cause the price to change — most professionals will tell you upfront that if hidden damage is found (rot behind trim, water damage behind a wall) the scope may need to be adjusted. That's fair. What's not fair is a surprise at the end with no prior conversation.

Watch for scope creep: A signed estimate isn't a blank check to expand the job. Any work outside the original scope should require a conversation and your approval before it happens. If a handyman starts doing additional work without discussing it first, that's a problem regardless of whether they charge for it.

Question 4: What's Your Experience With This Type of Repair?

Why it matters

Handyman is a broad term. Someone who's excellent at drywall patching may have no business touching deck framing. Someone who installs flooring every week may have limited experience with the electrical side of ceiling fan installation. Asking about specific experience with your specific job isn't rude — it's the right question.

This matters more for some jobs than others. Hanging a picture? Experience is barely a factor. Repairing a deck with structural concerns, matching a 30-year-old drywall texture, or diagnosing why a door is sticking due to foundation movement versus humidity — those require genuine experience to get right.

What a good answer looks like

A straightforward answer with specific examples. "Yes, I do deck repairs regularly — the most common issues I see are soft boards at the ledger connection and post bases that have rotted from ground contact." That kind of answer tells you they've actually done the work. A vague "oh yeah, I handle all kinds of things" without specifics is worth probing further.

Also a green flag: A handyman who tells you honestly when something is outside their wheelhouse. "I can replace fixtures but I don't do panel work" is the mark of someone who knows their limits — and that's exactly the person you want working in your home. Overconfidence in trades is how things go wrong.

Question 5: Do You Guarantee Your Work?

Why it matters

What happens if the repair fails? What happens if the paint bubbles a week later, the drywall patch cracks along the seam, or the door you paid to have adjusted is sticking again by fall? How a handyman answers this question tells you a lot about their confidence in their own work — and about whether they'll be reachable six weeks after the check clears.

A workmanship guarantee isn't about assuming the worst. Most repairs done correctly don't fail. But knowing that someone will stand behind their work changes the dynamic of the relationship. It means they have skin in the game beyond the initial paycheck.

What a good answer looks like

A clear statement of what's covered and for how long. "We guarantee our workmanship — if something we did fails, we'll come back and make it right." Understand the distinction between workmanship and materials: a handyman can guarantee that they installed a toilet correctly but can't guarantee the toilet itself won't develop a manufacturer defect. That's fair. What you want guaranteed is the quality of the labor.

A word of caution: An unlimited lifetime guarantee from someone you just found on a flyer is worth less than a reasonable, clearly defined guarantee from a professional with a track record in your area. The guarantee is only as good as the person standing behind it.

One More Thing: Are They Actually Local?

This isn't always on the checklist but it should be. A handyman based in your area — familiar with the housing stock, the climate, the specific quirks of homes in Moon Township and western Pennsylvania — brings context that someone from outside the area simply doesn't have. Response time is faster, follow-up is easier, and there's an extra layer of accountability that comes with working in the same neighborhood as your customers.
