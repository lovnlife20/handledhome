Shed Assembly FAQs

Does the shed need to be on a specific foundation?

Most kit sheds require a level base — either a gravel pad, concrete blocks, or a poured slab. We can assess your site and advise on what's needed, or handle simple gravel or block preparation as part of the job.

Do I need to be home during assembly?

You need to be available at the start to confirm placement and at the end to do a walkthrough. For larger kits we work independently once we get started.

How long does shed assembly take?

A standard 8x10 or 10x12 resin or metal kit shed typically takes 3–5 hours. Larger wood kit sheds or sheds requiring foundation work take longer. We'll give you an honest estimate based on your specific kit.

What if pieces are missing or damaged?

We'll inventory the kit when we arrive. If anything is missing or damaged, we'll help you identify what's needed so you can contact the manufacturer before we start assembly.

Shed Assembly in Moon Township and the Pittsburgh Area

Kit sheds are one of those projects that seems manageable until you open the box. The parts are heavy, the steps are sequential, and most instruction manuals assume two skilled assistants and a perfectly level concrete pad already in place. The reality for most Moon Township homeowners is a yard with some slope, one set of hands, and a Saturday that runs out before the roof panels go on. We handle kit shed assembly regularly and have the process down to something that gets done correctly in one visit.

The most common issues with DIY kit shed assembly are an unlevel foundation — which causes walls that won't square up and doors that don't close properly — skipped anchoring steps because the instructions made them sound optional, and hardware that was tightened by hand rather than torqued down. In western Allegheny County's clay-heavy soil, a shed that isn't properly anchored is one bad wind event away from shifting off its foundation. We don't skip the anchoring step.

When we assemble a kit shed, we start by preparing the site — a gravel base or block foundation that's level within manufacturer tolerance before the first panel goes up. We follow the build sequence in the instructions, check for square before committing any panel to its final position, and anchor the structure to the ground per the specs. Door alignment gets adjusted as the build progresses so it's right when the shed is complete, not something you're fighting with afterward.
