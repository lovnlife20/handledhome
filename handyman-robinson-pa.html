About Robinson Township

Robinson Township runs along the Route 60 corridor between Moon Township to the north and Carnegie to the south — a stretch of commercial development flanked by residential neighborhoods that filled in fast through the 1990s and 2000s. Townhome communities line the Route 60 corridor. Single-family neighborhoods run right up to Montour Run and the Montour Trail, which cuts through the center of the township and gives the area an unexpected amount of green space for how developed it is.

Airport proximity shaped a lot of Robinson's growth. Pittsburgh International is literally next door, which drew steady population from airline workers, business travelers, and people in the airport economy who wanted a short commute. That growth produced a housing stock that skews almost entirely toward the 1990s and 2000s — vinyl siding, composite decks, builder-grade fixtures, and construction from that era. Twenty-five years in, those homes are now asking for attention. The decks need boards replaced. The original doors and trim show their age. The caulking around windows that was never quite right has finally given out. The to-do list has been building.

Handled Home works in Robinson Township regularly because it's essentially our backyard. Moon Township is right next door, Coraopolis is to the east, and Robinson sits right in the middle of the Route 60 corridor we travel constantly. No travel premium, fast scheduling, and we know the housing stock cold.
