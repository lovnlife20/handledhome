Types of Flooring We Install

We install several flooring types in Moon Township and Pittsburgh-area homes. Here's what you need to know about each one.

Laminate Flooring

Laminate flooring is one of the most popular choices for Moon Township homeowners looking to update bedrooms, living rooms, and home offices. It offers a realistic hardwood look at a lower price point, installs as a floating floor, and holds up well in low-moisture rooms. Laminate flooring in Moon Township PA is particularly well-suited to finished areas that stay consistently dry — think bedrooms, dining rooms, and first-floor living areas. The key is keeping it away from areas with regular moisture exposure, which Pittsburgh's humidity can turn into a problem over time. When installed correctly with proper expansion gaps and the right underlayment, laminate flooring looks great and lasts for years.

Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)

LVP has become the go-to flooring choice for Pittsburgh-area homeowners who want a durable, waterproof option that handles real life. Its 100% synthetic core means spills, humidity, and moisture don't damage it — which matters in a climate like Pittsburgh's where summer humidity and seasonal temperature swings are significant. LVP installs as a floating floor over most subfloor types, handles minor subfloor imperfections better than most products, and is the right call for kitchens, basements, laundry rooms, and any space that sees moisture. It's also one of the most forgiving products to install cleanly, which keeps labor time and cost down.

Hardwood Flooring

Hardwood flooring — both solid and engineered — adds lasting value and a premium look that's hard to replicate with any other product. Solid hardwood is nailed or stapled to the subfloor and is best for above-grade rooms with stable humidity control. Engineered hardwood has a real wood veneer over a layered plywood core, making it more dimensionally stable and suitable for a wider range of conditions including some below-grade applications. In Pittsburgh's climate, engineered hardwood is generally the safer choice over solid hardwood unless the room stays well climate-controlled year-round. Both require a flat, clean subfloor and proper acclimation before installation.

Vinyl Sheet & Tile

Sheet vinyl and vinyl tile are practical choices for kitchens, bathrooms, laundry rooms, and utility spaces where full waterproofing is needed and budget matters. Sheet vinyl installs in one continuous piece with no seams, which means no gaps where water can work its way under the floor. Vinyl tile can be grouted for a tile-like look or installed with tight seams depending on the product. Both are durable, easy to clean, and handle the kind of moisture exposure that would damage laminate or hardwood over time.

Flooring Repairs

Not every flooring job is a full install. We repair and replace damaged sections of existing flooring where the material is still available, fix squeaky subfloor issues before they telegraph through new flooring, replace worn or broken transition strips, and address heaved or buckled planks from moisture exposure. If a flooring problem is isolated — a few damaged planks, a failed transition, a section that's lifting — a targeted repair is often all that's needed rather than replacing the entire floor.

Subfloor Prep

Subfloor preparation is the part of a flooring job that most homeowners don't think about until there's a problem. A floor installed over an uneven, soft, or damaged subfloor will show it — through movement, squeaks, and visible waves in the finished surface. We assess the subfloor before any flooring goes down, address soft spots, level low areas, remove residual adhesive from previous flooring, and make sure the surface is flat and solid. Getting the subfloor right is what makes the difference between a floor that looks good for years and one that causes problems from day one.

Common Flooring Problems Pittsburgh Area Homeowners Face

Pittsburgh's climate, housing stock, and soil conditions create specific flooring challenges. Here's what comes up most often.

Humidity Damage and Buckling

Pittsburgh summers are genuinely humid — and older homes without great climate control can swing dramatically between damp summers and dry winters. Laminate flooring in Moon Township homes that wasn't installed with proper expansion gaps, or that's exposed to regular humidity, often develops buckling and raised seams over time. LVP handles this far better, which is why we recommend it for most high-moisture or climate-variable areas.

Worn or Outdated Carpet

A large percentage of flooring calls we get in Moon Township, Coraopolis, and Robinson are homeowners ready to replace carpet that's been there since the house was built or last sold. Removing old carpet, addressing the subfloor underneath — which sometimes has staples, adhesive, or uneven spots — and installing new flooring is one of the most common full-room flooring jobs we do.

Water-Damaged or Peeling Vinyl

Older sheet vinyl in kitchens and bathrooms tends to peel at the edges and seams after years of moisture exposure. Once it starts lifting, water gets underneath and the subfloor deteriorates. Replacing worn sheet vinyl before the subfloor is compromised is the right call — waiting until the subfloor is soft turns a straightforward flooring replacement into a more involved repair.

Squeaky Subfloors

Squeaky floors are common in older Pittsburgh-area homes with wood subfloors. The squeak comes from the subfloor moving against joists or fasteners that have loosened over time. Putting new flooring over a squeaky subfloor doesn't fix the squeak — it usually transmits right through. We address subfloor squeaks before installation so the new floor goes down over a solid, quiet base.

Mismatched or Missing Transitions

Many homes in the Moon Township and Pittsburgh area have had flooring updated room by room over the years — which often leaves awkward, mismatched, or missing transitions between rooms and surfaces. We install or replace transition strips, thresholds, and T-moldings to create clean connections between flooring types and elevations throughout the house.

Outdated Flooring from Previous Owners

Buying a home and inheriting flooring choices from the previous owner is one of the most common reasons homeowners in Cranberry Township, Sewickley, and across the Pittsburgh area call us. Whether it's worn laminate, outdated vinyl, or carpet in rooms where it doesn't belong, updating the flooring is one of the highest-impact changes you can make to how a home looks and feels.

What to Expect When We Come Out for a Flooring Job

Here's how a typical flooring installation goes from first contact to finished floor.

1. Photos and Estimate

The fastest way to get an accurate estimate is to text us photos of the room, the existing flooring, any transitions and doorways, and the flooring material you've selected (or are considering). We review the photos, ask questions if needed, and give you a realistic estimate before we put anything on the schedule. This avoids surprises on installation day.

2. Subfloor Assessment

When we arrive, the first thing we do is assess the subfloor. We check for flatness, soft spots, residual adhesive from previous flooring, moisture issues, and anything else that needs to be addressed before the new floor goes down. If we find something that changes the scope of the job, we talk through it before starting — not halfway through installation.

3. Layout Planning

We plan the layout before the first plank goes down — how the flooring will run relative to the room's longest wall, where the starter row begins, and how cuts will land at doorways and walls. A well-planned layout looks natural when finished. A floor that wasn't planned can have awkward thin cuts at doorways or strips that look off.

4. Installation

We install per manufacturer specifications including required expansion gaps at walls and fixed objects, the correct underlayment for the product and subfloor type, and proper row staggering for a natural look. Cuts are made cleanly, planks are seated fully, and the floor is checked throughout installation for flatness and proper fit.

5. Trim and Transitions

When the flooring is down, we finish every edge and doorway — quarter round or base shoe at the walls, transition strips at every doorway where flooring types or elevations change, and thresholds at exterior doors. This is the finish work that separates a professional installation from a DIY result. Every edge gets attention.

6. Cleanup and Walkthrough

We clean up completely before we leave — no scraps, no dust, no packaging left behind. We walk through the finished floor with you, point out anything you should know about maintenance or care for the specific product installed, and make sure you're satisfied before we pack up. If something's not right, we address it before we go.

LVP vs Laminate — Which Is Right for Your Home?

Both are popular floating floor options, but they handle moisture very differently. Here's how to choose.

LVP (Luxury Vinyl Plank)

LVP has a 100% synthetic core, which means it's completely waterproof. Spills, humidity, and the occasional wet mop won't hurt it. It's the right call for kitchens, bathrooms, basements, laundry rooms, and any space that sees regular moisture. It also floats over the subfloor, which means it can handle minor subfloor imperfections and temperature-related movement without buckling.

Laminate

Laminate has a wood-fiber core that gives it a slightly more authentic underfoot feel compared to vinyl, but that core is not waterproof. It can swell at the seams when it gets wet repeatedly. Laminate is better suited for bedrooms, living rooms, and home offices that stay consistently dry and don't see spills or humidity regularly.

Pittsburgh Humidity Consideration

Pittsburgh summers are humid, winters are cold and dry, and that seasonal swing is more significant than a lot of homeowners realize. LVP handles that variation without issue. Laminate in a room that gets damp in July and dry in January can show movement at the seams over time. For most Moon Township homes, LVP is the lower-risk, lower-maintenance choice unless the room is consistently climate-controlled and stays dry year-round.

How Long Does Flooring Installation Take?

Timeline depends on room size, number of rooms, and what the subfloor looks like when we get there.

Single Room

Most single-room installs — a bedroom, home office, or smaller living area — are completed in one day. The room needs to be clear of furniture before we start, and the flooring material should already be on site and acclimated for at least 24–48 hours per the manufacturer's specs.

Multiple Rooms or Larger Projects

Multi-room projects and larger open-plan areas typically take two to three days. We'll give you a realistic timeline based on the total square footage and the layout before we schedule, so there are no surprises once the job is underway.

Subfloor Issues or Extensive Trim Work

If the subfloor needs leveling or has damaged sections, or if there's significant baseboard removal, reinstall, and trim work involved, that adds time to the project. We assess the subfloor and trim situation upfront so the schedule doesn't change halfway through the job.

Flooring Installation Cost in Moon Township

Several factors affect what flooring installation costs. Sending photos is the fastest way to get an accurate estimate for your specific situation.

Square Footage

More square footage means more material and more time. Room shape matters too — a simple rectangular room installs faster than a room with alcoves, closets, and irregular cuts. Total square footage is the starting point for any flooring estimate.

Flooring Material

LVP, laminate, engineered hardwood, solid hardwood, and tile all install differently and take different amounts of time. Some materials require more prep, more precise cuts, or specific subfloor conditions. The product you choose affects both the installation time and the overall project cost.

Subfloor Condition

A flat, clean, solid subfloor installs faster and cleaner than one that needs leveling, patching, or old flooring removed first. Subfloor problems that get discovered during installation add time and cost, which is why we like to see photos of the existing floor and discuss the subfloor situation before scheduling.

Furniture Moving

The room should be clear of furniture before installation begins. If furniture needs to be moved as part of the job, that should be discussed before scheduling so we can account for it in the time estimate. Moving heavy or large pieces adds time to the project.

Number of Transitions and Doorways

Each doorway and transition between rooms or surfaces requires a transition strip or threshold to be cut, fitted, and installed. A single open-plan room with no doorways is the simplest case. A floor that runs through multiple doorways and meets tile, carpet, or other flooring surfaces at several points takes more time to finish cleanly.

Baseboard Replacement

If the existing baseboards are in good shape, we can often remove and reinstall them around the new floor. If they're damaged, missing, or the new floor height requires new trim, new baseboard adds material and labor. We'll flag this when we review your photos so it's included in the estimate rather than coming up as a surprise on install day.

Flooring Installation FAQs

Do you install vinyl plank flooring?

Yes. We install vinyl plank flooring, including many click-lock and floating floor products.

Do you install laminate flooring?

Yes. We install laminate flooring in rooms, hallways, and smaller residential spaces.

Can the homeowner supply the flooring?

Yes. Homeowners can supply flooring materials. We recommend having extra material available for cuts, waste, and future repairs.

Do you handle baseboards and transitions?

Yes. We can discuss baseboards, quarter round, thresholds, and transition pieces as part of the project.

Can I text photos for an estimate?

Yes. Send photos of the room, existing flooring, transitions, doorways, and any flooring material you already purchased.

Do you install hardwood flooring?

Yes. We install both solid hardwood and engineered hardwood. Solid hardwood is nailed or stapled to the subfloor and is more sensitive to moisture and subfloor flatness. Engineered hardwood is more dimensionally stable and can install as a floating floor or glue-down depending on the product. Send us photos and let us know your subfloor type and we'll let you know if your situation is a good fit.

Can you install flooring over existing flooring?

Sometimes. Installing over existing flooring only works if the existing surface is flat, firmly secured, and doesn't put the new floor height in conflict with doorways, transitions, and thresholds. LVP over a sound, flat vinyl or tile floor is often doable. Installing over carpet or an already-elevated floor is generally not recommended. Send photos and we'll give you a straight answer before you commit to the approach.

How do I prepare my room before flooring installation?

Clear the room of furniture before we arrive. Have the flooring material on site and acclimated in the room for at least 24–48 hours beforehand — most manufacturers require this, and skipping it can cause the floor to shift after installation. If there are items in the room you need help moving, let us know in advance so we can plan for it.

Do you move furniture as part of the job?

Moving furniture is not automatically included — the room should be clear when we arrive. If you have specific pieces that need to be moved and you'd like us to handle that, let us know before we schedule so we can discuss it and account for the time. We want to avoid showing up to a full room on installation day.

Why Pittsburgh Basements Need Special Flooring Consideration

Pittsburgh basements are a different animal than basements in drier climates. The region sits at the confluence of three rivers, the soil holds moisture well, and summer humidity regularly climbs into the uncomfortable range. Even a basement that's never had visible water can have moisture migrating through the slab that will damage the wrong flooring product over time.

The most common mistake we see in Pittsburgh-area basements is installing laminate flooring below grade. Laminate has a wood-fiber core that absorbs moisture and swells at the seams — one wet event from a sump backup or a slow seep after a heavy rain, and the floor is done. We've replaced plenty of buckled basement laminate in Moon Township, Coraopolis, and Cranberry Township homes where LVP would have survived the same event without damage.

For most Pittsburgh basements, luxury vinyl plank is the right call. It's 100% waterproof, warm enough underfoot, installs over concrete with the right underlayment, and handles the humidity swings that cause problems with other products. Porcelain tile is also a solid basement choice for utility areas. Engineered hardwood can work in dry, consistently climate-controlled basements, but carries more risk than LVP with no real upside in most Pittsburgh homes.

Before any flooring goes down in a basement, we check for moisture. A simple tape test — plastic sheeting taped to the slab for 24–48 hours — tells you whether you have moisture coming through the concrete. If you do, that needs to be addressed before flooring is installed. Putting new flooring over a wet slab is the same job twice.
