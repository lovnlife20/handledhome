Robinson Township Decks Are Hitting the Age Where Things Fail

Robinson Township filled in fast between the mid-1990s and the mid-2000s. The townhome communities along the Route 60 corridor and the single-family neighborhoods running back toward Montour Run went up in a relatively short window, and nearly all of them came with a deck built the same way: pressure-treated pine framing, pressure-treated deck boards, 4x4 posts, and wooden railings.

That's a perfectly good way to build a deck. It just doesn't last forever. Pressure-treated pine in western Pennsylvania has a practical service life of roughly twenty to thirty years for the boards and railings, and the failures don't happen all at once — they show up one component at a time. A board goes soft near a joist. A railing post starts to move when you lean on it. The bottom stair tread flexes underfoot. Individually each one looks minor. Together they're the deck telling you it's reached that age.

We work on Robinson decks constantly because the whole township hit that mark at roughly the same time. That familiarity matters when we're giving you an estimate: we've usually seen your exact deck configuration before, on a house built by the same builder in the same decade.

Deck Problems We See Most Often in Robinson

Second-Story Townhome Decks

A lot of the townhome communities along Route 60 were built with elevated second-story walkout decks — smaller footprints, but supported on tall posts with a long stair run down to grade. Those tall posts and long stringers are where we find the most movement, because every bit of flex at the top of a post is magnified over eight or ten feet of height. Railings on elevated decks are also the ones most worth taking seriously, since a failure there is a fall from height rather than a step down to the lawn.

Ground-Level Decks Near Montour Run

Neighborhoods that back up toward Montour Run and the Montour Trail sit lower and hold more ground moisture, and it shows in the framing. Joist ends, post bases, and the bottom stair tread rot noticeably faster on those lots than on the higher ground closer to the Route 60 ridge. If your deck sits low to grade with limited airflow underneath, we pay particular attention to what's happening below the surface, not just on it.

Freeze-Thaw Damage

Pittsburgh's winter cycles are hard on wood decks. Water gets into an existing crack or a nail hole, freezes, expands, and makes the crack bigger — then does it again the next cold snap. That's why a deck that looked acceptable in October can have split boards and lifted fasteners by April. Spring is our busiest deck season in Robinson for exactly this reason, so if you're planning work for the summer, reaching out early in the season gets you a better spot on the schedule.

Adding Shade to an Existing Deck

A common follow-on request once a Robinson deck is repaired: shade. We handle pergola and gazebo kit installation over decks and patios — assembling, leveling, and anchoring a kit you've purchased. We don't design or custom-build pergolas, but if you've bought a kit and want it put up properly over a deck we've just made solid, that's a natural pairing.

Robinson Township Deck Repair FAQs

My deck is from the late 90s. Is it worth repairing or should I replace it?

It depends almost entirely on the framing. If the joists, beams, posts, and ledger are still solid — which on a lot of Robinson decks they are, because the framing sits shaded and dries faster than the sun-exposed surface — then replacing boards and railings over that structure gives you a deck that looks and feels new for a fraction of a full rebuild. If the framing itself has rot at the joist ends or a compromised ledger, repair stops making financial sense and we'll say so. We'll get under the deck and look before quoting either direction.

How quickly can you get out to Robinson Township?

Robinson is about five minutes from our base in Moon Township, so we're not building travel time into your price or your schedule. For most deck repairs we can usually get out within a few days to a week depending on the season. Spring and early summer are the busiest stretch — if you're planning deck work for warm weather, reaching out in late winter gets you a better spot.

Can you match my existing deck boards?

For pressure-treated pine, usually yes — the material is still widely available and new boards will weather to blend with the old ones over a season or two. They will look noticeably newer at first; that's unavoidable and we'll set that expectation upfront. For composite decking it depends on the brand and whether the line is still in production. If your composite is discontinued, we look for the closest current match and show you before ordering rather than surprising you at install.

Is my deck railing actually unsafe, or does it just feel loose?

Worth taking seriously either way. A railing should not move perceptibly when you put your weight against it. If it does, the connection has already loosened, and loosening is progressive — it doesn't stabilize on its own. On a ground-level deck a failure means a short fall. On the elevated townhome decks common along Route 60 it's a fall from height. If your railing moves, send us a photo of the post base and the rim connection and we'll tell you what we're looking at.

Do you do pressure washing and staining too?

We do pressure washing, and it's a sensible thing to schedule after board repairs so the whole deck gets cleaned at once. One caution specific to older pressure-treated decks: aggressive pressure washing on wood that's already softening will damage the surface further. We use appropriate pressure for the condition of the wood rather than blasting everything at maximum.

Do I need a permit for deck repair in Robinson Township?

Straight repairs — swapping boards, replacing railings, fixing stairs on an existing structure — generally don't require a permit. Structural changes, expanding the footprint, or a full rebuild typically do. Requirements are set by the township and do change, so confirm directly with the Robinson Township building department for your specific project. We'll tell you honestly when we think a job crosses from repair into territory where you should be making that call.
