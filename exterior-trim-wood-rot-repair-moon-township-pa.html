Act Early — Wood Rot Spreads

Wood rot is caused by moisture and fungus. Once it starts, it spreads into adjacent wood if left untreated. A small soft spot in a windowsill can work its way into the framing behind it.

Catching it early means a simple trim replacement. Ignoring it can mean a much larger repair. If you see peeling paint, soft or spongy wood, or discoloration around windows, doors, or roofline trim, it is worth getting it looked at.

Exterior Trim & Wood Rot FAQs

How do I know if I have wood rot?

Press the wood with a screwdriver or your finger. Soft, spongy, or crumbling wood is a sign of rot. Peeling paint or dark discoloration are also common indicators.

Can wood rot be repaired without full replacement?

Sometimes. Small areas of surface rot can be consolidated with epoxy wood filler. More extensive rot typically requires removing and replacing the affected board.

Does the repair include painting?

Repairs are primed and caulk-ready. Painting can be discussed depending on the scope of the job, but is not automatically included.

Can I send photos for an estimate?

Yes. Photos of the affected trim, close-ups of the rot, and a wider shot of the area help us assess the repair before scheduling a visit.

What materials do you use for replacement trim?

It depends on the location and exposure. For most exterior trim replacement — window casing, door trim, fascia — we use either cellular PVC trim board or primed pine, depending on what the situation calls for. PVC trim does not rot, does not absorb moisture, and holds paint well, which makes it the right call for high-exposure areas like window sills, door bases, and any horizontal surface where water can sit. For areas where matching the original wood profile matters — particularly in older homes where the trim has a specific character worth preserving — we use primed solid wood. We tell you which material we plan to use and why before we start, and we prime all six faces of wood trim before installation to slow future moisture absorption. We do not use cheap finger-jointed pine or unprimed material on exterior work.

How do I know if the rot has spread behind the trim into the sheathing?

We probe for this as part of every assessment. When we remove rotted trim, we check the sheathing and framing behind it for softness, discoloration, and moisture. Surface rot that was caught early usually has not reached the structural layer. Rot that has been present for several years — particularly around windows and doors where water has had a consistent entry point — sometimes has. If we find damage behind the trim, we tell you before doing additional work, explain the scope, and let you decide how to proceed. We do not cover up sheathing damage by installing new trim over it.

Do you work on older homes in Sewickley and similar communities where the original exterior woodwork is worth preserving?

Yes, and this is a meaningful part of our exterior trim work. Sewickley and communities like it have Victorian and colonial revival homes with original exterior profiles — wide casings, detailed corner boards, built-up cornices — that are worth repairing rather than replacing wholesale with something generic. We match profiles carefully, use solid wood where PVC trim board would look out of place, and take our time on these jobs. The goal is a repair that reads as original, not as a patch. If you have a historic or older home with exterior woodwork that needs attention, call us before assuming it all has to be replaced.

Wood Rot Repair in Moon Township Homes

Exterior wood rot is one of those problems that tends to start small and get ignored. A soft spot on a windowsill, a fascia board that's starting to give, a section of door trim that doesn't look quite right. The issue is that rotted wood doesn't stay contained — water that gets behind rotted trim continues working into the sheathing and framing behind it, and what started as a cosmetic problem can become a structural one over time. Catching and addressing wood rot early is always a better outcome than waiting.

Moon Township homes see a consistent amount of wood rot, particularly in the older neighborhoods. The combination of Pittsburgh's wet climate — significant rainfall, humid summers, and snow — and homes that are thirty, forty, or more years old creates predictable failure points: the bottom corners of window trim where water collects, fascia board ends that absorb moisture from gutters, and the base of exterior door frames that sit close to the ground. Newer homes aren't immune — improperly caulked joints around windows and doors let water in regardless of how recently the home was built.

When we come out, we probe the affected area to understand how far the damage extends and whether what we can see is the whole problem or just the visible part. We remove the rotted material, treat the exposed area, and replace it with new wood that's primed on all six faces before installation. Caulking and sealing at all joints is included — that's what prevents the new repair from starting the same cycle over again. If we find damage that extends beyond the trim itself, we tell you before doing additional work.
