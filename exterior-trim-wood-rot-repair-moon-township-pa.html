Act Early — Wood Rot Spreads

Wood rot is caused by moisture and fungus. Once it starts, it spreads into adjacent wood if left untreated. A small soft spot in a windowsill can work its way into the framing behind it.

Catching it early means a simple trim replacement. Ignoring it can mean a much larger repair. If you see peeling paint, soft or spongy wood, or discoloration around windows, doors, or roofline trim, it is worth getting it looked at.

How the Repair Process Works

1. Assess the Damage

We probe the affected wood to determine how far the rot extends and whether partial repair or full replacement is needed.

2. Remove & Replace

Rotted sections are removed and replaced with new trim cut to match. We use materials appropriate for exterior exposure.

3. Seal & Finish

The repaired area is caulked and sealed. The wood is left primed and ready for paint to complete the job.

Exterior Trim & Wood Rot FAQs

How do I know if I have wood rot?

Press the wood with a screwdriver or your finger. Soft, spongy, or crumbling wood is a sign of rot. Peeling paint or dark discoloration are also common indicators.

Can wood rot be repaired without full replacement?

Sometimes. Small areas of surface rot can be consolidated with epoxy wood filler. More extensive rot typically requires removing and replacing the affected board.

Does the repair include painting?

Repairs are primed and caulk-ready. Painting can be discussed depending on the scope of the job, but is not automatically included.

Can I send photos for an estimate?

Yes. Photos of the affected trim, close-ups of the rot, and a wider shot of the area help us assess the repair before scheduling a visit.
