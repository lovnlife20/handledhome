What a Seasonal Checkup Typically Covers

  • Caulking and weatherstripping around windows and doors
  • Outdoor faucet shutoff before winter
  • Gutters and drainage before heavy rain or snow
  • Small repairs that add up over a season
  • Door and window seals that let in drafts
  • General wear that shows up after winter
  • Deferred maintenance items on a running list
  • A general walk-through to catch what you might have missed

A Little Attention Twice a Year Goes a Long Way

Most home maintenance issues do not happen suddenly — they build up gradually across a season and get noticed all at once when the weather changes. A seasonal maintenance visit is about catching those small things — a gap in weatherstripping, a faucet that needs shutting off, a punch list of minor repairs — before they turn into bigger, more expensive problems.

We can do a single winterization or spring checkup, or set up both as a recurring visit so the house gets looked at twice a year without you having to remember to schedule it.

Seasonal Maintenance FAQs

What is included in a winterization visit?

Typically outdoor faucet shutoff, caulking and weatherstripping checks, and a general walk-through for anything that needs attention before cold weather.

Can you set up a recurring seasonal schedule?

Yes, many homeowners have us come out twice a year — once in fall and once in spring.

Do you fix things you find during the checkup?

Small items, yes, in the same visit. Larger repairs we will point out and quote separately.

Is this different from your caulking and weatherproofing service?

Seasonal maintenance is broader — caulking is one part of it, along with a general home walk-through and other small maintenance items.

Seasonal Home Maintenance in Moon Township

Pittsburgh's weather swings hard between seasons, and homes take the toll from both ends — freeze-thaw cycles in winter and heavy rain in spring and summer. A seasonal maintenance visit is about staying ahead of that cycle rather than reacting to a problem after it has already caused damage.

Fall visits typically focus on getting ready for cold weather — shutting off outdoor faucets, checking weatherstripping, and making sure gutters are clear before snow arrives. Spring visits are more about assessing what winter left behind and catching up on anything that needs attention before summer.
