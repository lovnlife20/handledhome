Signs Your Exterior Shutters Need to Be Replaced

Shutters take a beating from Pittsburgh weather. Here's what to look for when it's time to replace them.

Fading and Chalking

Vinyl shutters lose their color over time, especially on south- and west-facing exposures. When they fade to a chalky, washed-out version of the original color — and no amount of cleaning brings it back — replacement is the right move. Shutters that no longer match the trim color make the whole front of the house look tired.

Cracking or Warping

Freeze-thaw cycles in western Pennsylvania are hard on vinyl. Shutters that show visible cracks, splits along the louvers, or warping that causes them to bow away from the wall aren't just cosmetic problems — gaps and cracks let moisture in behind the shutter and against the siding. Replace before that becomes a bigger issue.

Missing Shutters

A shutter that's blown off or gone missing makes the front of a house look incomplete in a way that's hard to ignore. One missing shutter on a window that previously had two is immediately noticeable. Sourcing a match and replacing just the missing piece is often straightforward if the style is still available.

Pulling Away from the House

Shutters that are visibly tilting, pulling away from the wall, or hanging at an angle have a fastener failure — either the original screws have loosened over the years or the mounting surface behind them has deteriorated. A shutter that's starting to separate will eventually fall, and the hardware hole it leaves is worth addressing before then.

Mounting Shutters on Any Surface

The right installation depends on what the shutters are going into. We use the appropriate fasteners and technique for each surface.

Vinyl Siding

Vinyl siding is the most common installation surface in Moon Township, and also one of the easier ones to get wrong. Standard screws driven straight into vinyl without backing will loosen over time as the siding expands and contracts with temperature. We mount through the siding to a solid backing behind it, ensuring the shutters stay tight through Pittsburgh winters and summers alike.

Brick

Brick requires masonry anchors — tapcon screws driven into the mortar joints or the brick face, depending on the situation. This is a clean, permanent installation when done correctly. The key is using the right bit, the right anchor size, and avoiding cracking the brick face by driving into a weak mortar joint without proper prep.

Wood

Wood siding and trim boards are straightforward to mount into — the main consideration is using exterior-rated fasteners that won't rust and stain the surface, and making sure the shutter is flush against the wood rather than bridging a contour. We pre-drill to avoid splitting older or dry wood siding.

Stucco

Stucco requires masonry anchors similar to brick, plus care not to crack the stucco face in the process. Older stucco especially can be fragile at the surface. We use smaller pilot holes and appropriate anchors to get a secure mount without damaging the finish around the fastener.

Exterior Shutter FAQs

Do you supply the shutters or do I need to buy them?

Either works. We can source shutters for you — particularly useful when matching an existing style or sizing for specific windows — or we can install shutters you've already purchased. Let us know which situation you're in and we'll plan accordingly.

How do you mount shutters on brick?

Brick installation uses masonry anchors — typically tapcon screws into the mortar joints or brick face. We drill pilot holes with a masonry bit, set the appropriate anchor, and drive the fastener cleanly. When done right, this is a permanent and very secure mount.

Can you match shutters I already have on the house?

In most cases yes, especially for common styles like flat panel or louvered vinyl shutters. If the exact style is still in production, finding a match is straightforward. If the original shutters are discontinued, we'll find the closest match available and discuss it with you before ordering.

How long does shutter installation take?

Most jobs are done in a single visit. A full house replacement — removing old shutters and installing new ones on all windows — typically takes a few hours depending on the number of windows and the surface type. A few replacement shutters on a partial job can often be done in under an hour.

My shutters keep coming loose — can you fix that?

Yes. Shutters that repeatedly come loose usually have a fastener problem — the wrong fastener for the surface, or the fastener was driven into hollow backing that can't hold a screw over time. We'll assess what's behind the shutter and remount with hardware that will actually hold.
