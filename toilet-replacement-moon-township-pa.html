Toilet Replacement FAQs

Do I need to supply the toilet?

Yes — you purchase the toilet you want and we handle the installation. We include the wax ring, supply line, and bolts needed for the job. Just have the toilet (still in the box) ready when we arrive.

Is old toilet removal and disposal included?

Yes. We disconnect and remove the old toilet and haul it away so you don't have to deal with it.

How long does toilet replacement take?

Most toilet replacements take about an hour. We shut off the water, remove the old toilet, inspect the flange, set the new toilet on a fresh wax ring, reconnect the supply, and test everything before we leave.

What if the toilet flange is damaged?

We check the flange when we pull the old toilet. If there's a problem, we'll tell you what we found and what the options are before proceeding. Most flange issues can be addressed in the same visit.

Toilet Replacement in Moon Township and the Pittsburgh Area

Toilet replacement looks like a straightforward swap until the old toilet comes off the floor. Corroded closet bolts that won't loosen, a wax ring that's been there for twenty-plus years, a flange that's been raised or lowered by successive floor renovations — these are all things we run into regularly in Moon Township homes. Having someone who knows how to handle them means the job gets done in one visit without a mid-job hardware store run.

Toilets get replaced for a few different reasons in Pittsburgh-area homes. Running toilets that waste water and drive up utility bills, cracked tanks or bases, porcelain that's been discolored by hard water over decades of use, and upgrades to a dual-flush or comfort-height model are all common calls. In older Moon Township homes, the toilet may be the original one from when the house was built, and the floor flange underneath has some history that affects how the replacement needs to be approached.

When we do a toilet replacement, we shut off the supply, flush and sponge out the remaining water, disconnect the supply line, and remove the old toilet. The wax ring is always replaced — we don't reuse old wax rings. We check the flange and address any issues before the new toilet goes down. The new toilet gets set with a fresh wax ring, closet bolts tightened evenly, and the supply line connected with new braided stainless. We flush several times and check for leaks at the base and supply connection before the job is done. You supply the toilet, we handle the rest.
