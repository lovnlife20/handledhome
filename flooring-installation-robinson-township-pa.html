The Most Common Flooring Job in Robinson Township

Robinson Township's residential neighborhoods went up in a concentrated stretch between the mid-1990s and the mid-2000s, and the builders of that era finished nearly all of them the same way: carpet through the main living areas, bedrooms, and often the stairs, with sheet vinyl in the kitchen, laundry, and baths. It was the standard package.

Carpet has a realistic life of ten to fifteen years. That means the original flooring in most Robinson homes is now well past replacement age, and the majority of the flooring calls we take in the township are the same project: pull the worn builder carpet, prep what's underneath, and install luxury vinyl plank. It's become the default upgrade for this housing stock, and for good reason — it handles Pittsburgh's humidity swings, it stands up to pets and kids, and it changes how a room reads more than almost any other single change.

Because we do this job in Robinson so often, we know what's under the carpet before we pull it: OSB subfloor from that construction era, a field of staples that all have to come out, and occasionally a squeak or two that needs to be addressed before anything new goes down.

Flooring Situations Specific to Robinson Homes

Basements Toward Montour Run

The neighborhoods sitting lower toward Montour Run and the Montour Trail have basements that deal with more ground moisture than the homes up on higher ground near the Route 60 ridge. For any below-grade room in Robinson our recommendation is LVP without hesitation — it has a fully synthetic core and won't swell if it meets water. Laminate in a Robinson basement is a gamble we'd steer you away from, because when a laminate core gets wet it swells at the seams and the damage is permanent. Read more: Best Flooring for Pittsburgh Basements →

Townhome Open Main Floors

The townhome layouts along the Route 60 corridor typically run one continuous main level — entry, living area, dining, and kitchen with few full walls between them. That's ideal for flooring visually, but it means the whole level reads as a single surface, so plank direction, layout planning, and where the seams land actually matter. We plan the layout before the first plank goes down rather than starting at a wall and hoping it works out at the far end.

Stairs and Landings

Robinson's two-story and townhome layouts mean stairs come up constantly. When new flooring meets a staircase, stair nosing has to cap the edge for a safe, finished transition. We cut and fit nosing to match the flooring and secure it so it doesn't shift underfoot. If you're keeping carpet on the treads and running hard flooring at the top and bottom, we handle that junction cleanly too.

Piecemeal Transitions

A very common find in Robinson homes: flooring updated one room at a time over twenty years, leaving a collection of mismatched, broken, or missing transition strips at every doorway. We cut and install T-moldings, reducers, end caps, and thresholds with the right profile at each change so the whole floor reads finished instead of assembled in stages.

Subfloor Prep After Carpet

Pulling carpet in a home of this age leaves hundreds of staples and tack strip that all have to come out before a floating floor goes down. Skip that and you get high spots that telegraph through the new floor. We also check for movement while the subfloor is exposed — a squeak that exists under carpet will transmit right through new flooring if it isn't screwed down first. Read more: How to Fix Squeaky Floors →

Replacing Damaged Sections

A few planks buckled after a dishwasher leak or a section pulled up during a plumbing repair. If the material is still available we replace just the affected area and blend the seam. If it's discontinued we look for the closest current match and show it to you before ordering — and we'll say honestly when replacing the full room is the cleaner outcome than an obvious patch.

Robinson Township Flooring FAQs

Do you supply the flooring or do I?

You supply it — you pick the brand, style, and color, and we install it, with no markup from us on materials. One thing to plan for: buy 10–15% more than your measured square footage to cover cuts, waste, and any future repairs. We'll confirm the quantity looks right before we start. If you'd like recommendations before buying, text us the room dimensions and what the room is used for and we're glad to suggest options.

Should I put LVP or laminate in my Robinson basement?

LVP, essentially always. Laminate has a wood-fiber core that swells permanently if it gets wet, and basements in this area — particularly in the lower neighborhoods toward Montour Run — see enough seasonal humidity and the occasional water event that we don't consider laminate a safe bet below grade. LVP's synthetic core handles moisture without failing. The cost difference is modest and the risk difference is not.

Can you install over the existing sheet vinyl in my kitchen?

Often yes, if the existing vinyl is flat, firmly bonded, and in one layer — that's a common and perfectly sound approach in homes of this era. The thing to check is height: adding a layer raises the finished floor, which affects door clearance, appliance height under counters, and how the transition meets the next room. We look at all of that before recommending it. Going over carpet, or over laminate that's already floating, is not something we'd recommend.

How long does a room take?

Most single rooms are done in a day including subfloor prep and trim. An open townhome main level or a large great room may run a day and a half to two days depending on square footage and how many doorways and transitions are involved. Multi-room jobs we schedule and timeline before starting. Two things need to be true when we arrive: the room is clear of furniture, and the flooring has been on site acclimating for at least 24 hours per the manufacturer's spec.

Do you move the furniture?

The room should be clear when we get there — furniture moving isn't automatically part of the job. If you have a few heavy pieces you need help with, tell us before we schedule so we can plan the time into the estimate. We'd rather sort that out in advance than arrive to a room that isn't ready and lose the day for both of us.

What about the baseboards?

New flooring changes the reveal at the bottom of the wall, so baseboards have to be dealt with either way. We can pull existing baseboards and reinstall them after the floor is in, or leave them and finish with quarter round or base shoe to cover the expansion gap. Pulling and reinstalling gives the cleaner look; quarter round is faster and less disruptive to the paint. We'll walk you through the tradeoff and let you choose.
