Note on Electrical Work

Smart home device installation typically involves low-voltage wiring — doorbell wires, thermostat wires, and data cables — which falls within standard handyman scope.

New circuit installation, panel work, or anything requiring a licensed electrician is outside our scope and we will let you know upfront if your project requires it.

Smart Home Installation FAQs

Do I need to buy the devices first?

Yes, you purchase the devices and we install them. If you are unsure which model to get, let us know what you are trying to do and we can help point you in the right direction.

Do you set up the app too?

Basic app setup and device pairing is included. Full network configuration or troubleshooting complex home automation setups may be outside scope for a standard visit.

My old thermostat doesn't have a C wire. Is that a problem?

Many smart thermostats include a C-wire adapter that solves this. We check your existing wiring first and work with what's there.

Can you install multiple devices in one visit?

Yes. Installing a thermostat, doorbell, and a couple of cameras in a single visit is a common and efficient way to do it.

Smart Home Installation for Moon Township and Pittsburgh Homeowners

Smart home devices are easy to order and not always easy to get working correctly. A smart thermostat that needs a C-wire your HVAC system doesn't have. A video doorbell that needs to tie into existing wiring or use a specific mount for your door frame style. A smart lock that requires different door prep than your current hardware. Security cameras that need to be positioned, wired, and connected to a network in a way that actually covers what you want to cover. These are problems we solve regularly for homeowners across Moon Township and the Pittsburgh area.

Smart home upgrades in Moon Township come in a few flavors. Newer homes off Route 60 and University Boulevard often have homeowners who bought devices with the intent of installing them and never got around to it — the boxes are on a shelf somewhere. In older homes, the calls are more often about replacing dated thermostats or doorbells with modern equivalents, and the older wiring adds complications that need to be worked around. Either situation is familiar territory for us.

When we install smart home devices, we start by assessing what's already in place — existing wiring, mounting locations, network access, and compatibility with what the homeowner has. We mount, wire, configure, and test every device before leaving. For smart thermostats, that includes confirming C-wire availability or installing an adapter if needed. For smart locks and doorbells, we adjust the door hardware or doorbell mount as needed. We walk you through how to use everything before we're done.
