Paint-Ready Does Not Always Mean Painted

Most drywall repairs include patching, taping, mudding, sanding, and leaving the repaired area ready for primer and paint.

Painting can sometimes be included depending on the job, but it should be discussed up front. Matching existing wall paint is not always perfect, especially if the paint has faded, aged, or was not recently applied.

How to Know If Your Drywall Needs Professional Repair

Some drywall issues are easy DIY fixes. These ones usually aren't — and doing them wrong just means doing them twice.

Cracks Wider Than a Hairline

Thin hairline cracks are often just paint or settling. Cracks you can fit a fingernail into are moving — that usually means the tape underneath has failed or the drywall is flexing, and just filling it with spackle won't hold.

Cracks That Come Back in the Same Spot

If you've patched a crack and it came back within a few months, something is causing it to reopen — seasonal movement, a failed tape joint, or an underlying structural issue. A proper repair addresses the cause, not just the symptom.

Water Stains or Discoloration

A brown ring or discolored patch on the wall or ceiling means water got in. Once the leak is fixed, the drywall needs to be assessed. Water-soaked drywall loses its integrity and can grow mold — it often needs to be cut out and replaced, not just painted over.

Patches That Keep Showing Through Paint

If you've repainted a patched area and it keeps telegraphing through — either as a color difference or a texture difference — the patch wasn't finished correctly. Proper mudding, feathering, and priming prevents this.

Patch vs. Replace — What’s the Right Call

Not every drywall problem needs new sheet — but some do. Here's how we think about it.

When a Patch Works

Small to medium holes, nail pops, surface cracks, and minor cutouts are ideal for patching. The surrounding drywall is structurally sound, the area is dry, and there's no mold involved. A good patch, properly finished, is invisible under paint.

When a Section Needs to Be Cut Out

If there's visible mold, the drywall is water-soaked and spongy, the damage covers a large area, or the panel is sagging or crumbling, a patch won't hold. We cut back to solid material, install a proper backer, and replace the section cleanly.

Drywall Repair FAQs

Can you repair small drywall holes?

Yes. We handle small holes, doorknob damage, dents, cracks, and other common drywall repairs.

Do you repair ceilings?

Yes. We repair ceiling cracks, holes, damaged drywall sections, and some water-damage areas once the leak has been fixed.

Is painting included?

Not automatically. We can discuss painting depending on the repair, but most drywall work is quoted as paint-ready unless painting is specifically included.

Can I text photos for an estimate?

Yes. Photos help a lot. Send close-up photos and a wider shot of the wall or ceiling so we can understand the repair better.

How long does drywall repair take to dry before painting?

It depends on how many coats of joint compound were applied and your home's humidity level. A small patch with one or two coats typically needs 24 hours of drying time before it can be sanded and primed. Larger repairs with multiple coats may need 24 to 48 hours between coats. We'll let you know where things stand when we finish so you know when to have your painter come in.

Can you match my existing wall texture?

For common textures like orange peel and light knockdown, we can usually get a close match. Heavily hand-applied or custom textures are harder to replicate exactly. We'll tell you upfront if the texture on your wall is something we can match well versus something that may still show a slight difference under raking light.

Do you repair drywall in bathrooms or high-moisture areas?

Yes, with a caveat. We use moisture-resistant drywall (commonly called green board or cement board depending on the application) in areas like bathrooms and laundry rooms. If the damage was caused by an ongoing leak, the leak needs to be fixed before we repair the drywall — patching over an active moisture problem won't hold and can lead to mold.

What to Expect From a Drywall Repair at Handled Home

Drywall repair sounds simple but goes wrong quickly if it's rushed. The patch is only part of the job — the work that makes a repair invisible under paint is in how the compound is applied. Thin coats, edges feathered wide enough to blend with the surrounding wall, and proper drying time between coats are what separate a repair that disappears from one that telegraphs through every finish coat you put over it. We've fixed a lot of patches that other people did before us, which gives us a clear picture of where DIY drywall repairs typically fall short.

In Moon Township homes, drywall calls come from a few recurring situations. Doorknobs that punched through walls are the most common single repair. Settling cracks in older homes — especially at window and door corners in areas like Carnot and along Flaugherty Run — are trickier because they tend to come back if you just fill them without addressing the underlying tape joint. Water damage from a roof issue or a plumbing repair that's since been fixed is another frequent one; that category usually involves cutting out the damaged section rather than patching over it.

When we come out, we assess what we're dealing with and tell you whether a patch will hold or whether the section needs to be cut out and replaced. Small holes and surface cracks are typically a single visit — patch, mud, tape if needed, sand, and leave it paint-ready. Larger repairs, ceiling work, and areas that need texture can take more time or a follow-up visit after the compound dries fully. We let you know up front so there are no surprises on either end.
