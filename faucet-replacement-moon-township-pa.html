Faucet Replacement FAQs

Do I need to supply the faucet?

Yes — you purchase the faucet you want and we install it. That way you get the exact style, finish, and features you want. Have the faucet on hand before we arrive and we'll take care of everything else.

What if the pipes or connections under the sink are corroded?

We'll let you know what we find. Corroded shutoff valves and supply lines are common and we can replace those at the same time. If something more involved is needed, we'll tell you clearly before proceeding.

How long does faucet replacement take?

Most kitchen or bathroom faucets take 30–60 minutes. Batching a kitchen faucet with a bathroom faucet in one visit is efficient and saves a service call.

Do you test for leaks before leaving?

Always. We run the water, check all connections under the sink, and confirm everything is sealed before we pack up.

Faucet Replacement in Moon Township and Pittsburgh Area Homes

Faucet replacement is more involved than it looks from the outside. The shutoff valves under the sink may not have been turned in twenty years and don't always cooperate. Supply lines corrode and the fittings can be difficult to break free. The drain assembly needs to come out with the old faucet and get refitted to the new one. And in older homes — common across Moon Township — plumbing connections are sometimes slightly off-standard and require adapters or workarounds that aren't included in the box with the new faucet.

The most common faucet replacement calls in Moon Township are kitchen upgrades — homeowners who've bought a pull-down sprayer or a new handle style and don't want to deal with the under-cabinet gymnastics required to install it — and bathroom vanity faucets where the drain stopper assembly also needs to be swapped. We also get calls from homeowners dealing with faucets that drip constantly or leak at the base, where replacement makes more sense than repair because the fixture is already worn out.

When we do a faucet replacement, we shut off the supply lines and confirm the shutoffs are actually working. Old supply lines come out and get replaced with new braided stainless — this isn't optional, because installing a new faucet on old corroded lines just moves the potential leak point downstream. The new faucet goes in per the manufacturer's instructions, the drain assembly gets reconnected and adjusted, and we run a full test cycle before calling the job done: hot, cold, spray function if applicable, and a leak check under pressure at the supply connections.
