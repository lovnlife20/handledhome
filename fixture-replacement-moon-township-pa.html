Fixture Replacement FAQs

Do I need to supply the new fixture?

Yes — you purchase the fixture you want and we install it. That way you get exactly what you want without any guesswork.

Can you replace a light fixture if there's no existing wiring?

We work with existing wiring and junction boxes. For new wiring runs, you would need a licensed electrician. Most replacements use existing wiring.

How long does fixture replacement take?

Most single fixture swaps take under an hour. Batching several fixtures in one visit is efficient and a good use of the trip.

Do you do multiple fixtures in one visit?

Yes, and it makes sense to. Many homeowners have a list of fixtures that need replacing — we can knock them all out in one trip.

Fixture Replacement for Moon Township Homeowners

Fixture replacement — light fixtures, ceiling fans, faucets, toilets, and other household hardware — is the kind of work that often sits on homeowners' lists longer than it should. The old fixture works well enough, but it looks dated or came with the house and nobody ever liked it. The new one is sitting in a box. The job doesn't need a licensed electrician or plumber, but it does require knowing what you're doing to do it cleanly and safely. That's where Handled Home comes in.

In Moon Township, fixture replacement calls most commonly involve light fixtures being swapped after a kitchen or bathroom refresh, ceiling fans being replaced with something that doesn't wobble or make noise, and toilets or faucets being updated to match a bathroom renovation. Older Moon Township homes often have dated fixture choices — brass hardware from the '90s, fluorescent flush-mount ceiling fixtures from the '80s — that homeowners have been meaning to update for years. Newer homes get calls for a different reason: builder-grade fixtures that were fine to close on but are being upgraded now that the homeowner has settled in.

When we do fixture replacements, we confirm the power is off before touching any wiring, check that the existing junction box is rated for what's being installed, and make the wiring connections correctly before the fixture gets mounted. For plumbing fixtures, we turn off the supply, swap old supply lines for new ones, and test for leaks before calling the job done. We clean up and dispose of the old fixture before we leave. A list of multiple replacements in one visit is common and usually more efficient for everyone.
