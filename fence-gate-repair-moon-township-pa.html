A Fence Should Do Its Job Without a Second Thought

A fence and gate are supposed to be background details you do not think about — until a post rots out, a gate stops latching, or a section leans further every season. Most fence repairs are localized to a section or two rather than the whole fence line.

We check post condition, hardware, and panel damage before recommending a repair, and replace only what is actually failing.

Fence & Gate FAQs

Do you repair wood, vinyl, and chain link fences?

Yes, we repair all three, along with the gates and hardware that go with them.

Can you fix just one section instead of the whole fence?

Yes, most repairs are localized to the damaged posts, panels, or pickets rather than the entire fence line.

Why does my gate keep dragging or not latching?

Usually a settling or leaning post, worn hinges, or a shifted latch plate — all things we can adjust or repair.

Can you replace a single rotted post?

Yes, replacing individual posts is one of our most common fence repair requests.

Can you add a small new section to my existing fence?

Yes, we can build and tie in a small new section to match an existing fence line. We do not take on full new fence installations, but small additions and extensions are something we handle regularly.

Fence and Gate Repair in Moon Township Homes

Fence posts are usually the first thing to fail, whether from rot at the ground line, frost heave, or simple age. Once a post starts leaning, the sections attached to it follow, which is why a fence that looked fine last year can suddenly look noticeably crooked. Catching a post problem early keeps the repair to one or two sections instead of a longer stretch.

Gates get more daily use than any other part of a fence, so hinges wear out and latch plates shift out of alignment faster than the rest of the fence line. A gate that drags on the ground or will not catch the latch is almost always a quick fix once we identify what has shifted.
