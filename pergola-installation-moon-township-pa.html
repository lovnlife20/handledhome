Why Proper Pergola Assembly Matters

A pergola kit that is not properly leveled, squared, or anchored can rack, lean, and work its hardware loose over time — especially under Pittsburgh wind and the freeze-thaw cycles that move posts set in the ground.

Getting the base level and the posts anchored correctly from the start keeps the structure solid for years. We assemble the kit you provide according to the manufacturer's instructions and anchor it per the kit specs and your site. We do not design or custom-build pergolas, and projects that involve footings, permits, electrical, or attachment to the home's structure may need additional evaluation before work begins.

Pergola Installation FAQs

Do you build custom pergolas?

No — we focus on assembling and installing customer-supplied pergola kits. If you've purchased a kit, whether wood, vinyl, aluminum, or cedar, we handle the assembly, leveling, and anchoring. We don't design or fabricate pergolas from scratch.

Do I need to buy the pergola kit myself?

Yes. The homeowner supplies the kit. We're happy to talk through placement and what to look for before you buy, but the structure we install is the kit you provide.

Can you anchor a pergola to my deck or concrete patio?

Yes. We anchor pergola posts to decks, concrete patios, or the ground using the hardware that comes with the kit and the right method for your surface and site conditions.

How long does pergola kit assembly take?

It depends on the size and material. A smaller freestanding kit can take a few hours; a larger pergola, or one anchored into concrete or attached to the house, can run most of a day. We give you a time estimate when you request a quote.

Do pergolas need a permit in Moon Township?

It depends on the size, whether it's attached to the house, and whether it requires footings. Some outdoor structures require permits in Pennsylvania. We recommend checking with Moon Township directly before the project starts.

Can I text photos for an estimate?

Yes. Photos of the spot where the pergola will go — and the kit box or model details if you have them — help us give you a much more accurate estimate before we come out.

Pergola Kit Installation in Moon Township and the Pittsburgh Area

A pergola kit looks straightforward on the box and a lot more involved once the bundles of posts, beams, and rafters are stacked in your garage. Kits from the big-box stores and online retailers have improved over the years, but the instruction booklets still assume a perfectly level base, a second set of hands, and a free weekend — and they come with a hardware bag full of brackets, bolts, and connectors that all need to go in the right place and in the right order. Getting it assembled to spec, level, square, and properly anchored is the kind of project that eats a Saturday and a Sunday for one person working alone. Most Moon Township homeowners would rather spend that time actually sitting under the thing.

The problems that come up most often with DIY pergola installs are bases that weren't leveled before assembly started, posts that weren't anchored adequately for our climate, and hardware installed out of sequence that can't be corrected without taking sections back apart. A pergola that isn't anchored well shifts and racks over time — something that's especially pronounced in the clay-heavy soils common across parts of western Allegheny County, which expand and contract with moisture and frost. Add in the wind exposure that comes with an open backyard structure, and proper anchoring stops being optional.

When we install a pergola, we start with what you've already bought. We assess the site and the surface the pergola will sit on — deck, patio, or ground — confirm it's level and square, and then assemble the kit following the manufacturer's instructions throughout. Anchoring is done per the kit specs and your site conditions, and we go through the visible hardware to confirm everything is properly fastened before we call it done. We're assembling and installing the kit you provide, not designing or custom-building a structure — and if your project involves footings, a permit, electrical, or attaching to the house, we'll let you know if it needs additional evaluation first.
